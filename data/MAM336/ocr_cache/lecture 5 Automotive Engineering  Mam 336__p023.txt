Dual-Clutch Transmission (DCT)
Operating steps
1. The vehicle moves in first gear through Clutch 1.
2. Second gear is preselected on the other gear shaft.
3. When the control unit requests an upshift, Clutch 1 begins disengaging.
4. Simultaneously, Clutch 2 begins engaging.
5. Engine torque transfers from the first gear path to the second gear path.
6. Third gear can then be preselected for the next shift.
This overlapping clutch operation enables very fast gear changes with minimal interruption of wheel torque.
Advantages: Rapid shifting, high mechanical efficiency, and good acceleration.
Disadvantages: Complex mechanical and electronic control systems, potentially expensive repairs, and
possible low-speed hesitation or clutch wear in some designs.
